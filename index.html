Product Copy

Kids Building Blocks — Product Description Rewrite

⚠ The Problem

The original product description read like a spec sheet. It listed dimensions, materials, and age ranges — then stopped. Parents landing on the page had no emotional reason to buy. The copy answered "what is it?" but never "why does my child need this?" The result: high traffic, low add-to-cart rate.

⚙ The Fix

I shifted the frame from product features to parental outcomes. The new copy opened with the moment a parent wants to create — not the toy they're buying. I rewrote the headline to speak to the parent's desire ("Watch them build confidence, one block at a time"), restructured the bullet points to lead with benefits, and closed with a low-friction trust statement. Every sentence earned its place.

↑ The Result

The rewritten page reduced bounce rate by an estimated 30% and increased add-to-cart clicks in testing feedback. More importantly, the brand owner reported that customers were now leaving reviews that echoed the copy's language — a strong signal that the messaging finally resonated with real buyers.

Landing Page

SaaS Landing Page — Headline Optimization

⚠ The Problem

The landing page's headline was a classic founder mistake: it described what the tool does, not what the user gets. "AI-powered task management for modern teams" tells me nothing about my life after I sign up. Visitors weren't connecting with it. The page had a solid design but the headline wasn't pulling its weight — and the headline is the one element that determines whether someone reads the next line.

⚙ The Fix

I ran a copy audit and identified the core desire behind every sign-up: people don't want a task manager, they want to stop feeling overwhelmed. I rewrote three headline variants, each targeting a different angle — urgency, aspiration, and pain relief. The winning variant led with the end state: the feeling of being in control. Subheadline was tightened to handle objections immediately, and the CTA was changed from "Get Started" (vague) to "See It In Action" (low commitment, high curiosity).

↑ The Result

Scroll depth increased noticeably in user session recordings, and click-through on the primary CTA improved by an estimated 22% based on A/B test projection. The founder noted that demo requests started referencing the exact headline language — proof that the message finally matched what buyers actually wanted to hear.
